# Problem statement

## Title

MoneyWise SA — Building financial confidence, one decision at a time.

## Context

South Africa has broad access to banking, credit and mobile money, yet financial
outcomes remain poor for many young people. Access to financial services does not
automatically produce the confidence, knowledge or tools to make good financial
decisions.

Banks and regulators have highlighted the gap between financial access and financial
literacy. A young person can hold a bank account, a store card and a payroll deposit,
and still not know whether they can afford a R1,500 phone contract.

## The moment that defines the problem

A first salary arrives:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Salary | R8,000 |
| Rent | R2,500 |
| Transport | R1,200 |
| Groceries | R1,500 |
| Data | R400 |
| Family support | R500 |
| Entertainment | R400 |
| **Total expenses** | **R6,500** |
| **Remaining** | **R1,500** |

The person then asks:

- "Can I afford this?"
- "How much should I save?"
- "Why does my money disappear so quickly?"
- "How do I start an emergency fund?"
- "What is the difference between saving and investing?"

## Why current tools fall short

Existing financial apps are good at **reporting**: balances, transactions, charts of
what already happened. They are weak at **understanding**: what the numbers mean,
what to do next, and how confident someone should feel about their decisions.

The gap is not another expense tracker. The gap is financial confidence.

## Solution

MoneyWise SA is a mobile-first financial education and money-management web app that
pairs simple money tools with explanations:

1. **Financial dashboard** — income in, expenses out, what is left, and a plain
   language verdict ("You're currently spending less than you earn").
2. **Budget planner** — per-category limits with overspend flags
   ("Entertainment budget exceeded by R50").
3. **Money Confidence Score** — a 0–100 score across budgeting, saving, debt
   management, emergency fund and financial knowledge. It measures habits, never
   wealth.
4. **Learning hub** — short lessons on budgeting, banking, credit, saving,
   investing, scams and loans, plus a knowledge check.
5. **Calculators** — savings, emergency fund, debt repayment and a 50/30/20 split.
6. **Savings goals** — target, current amount, monthly contribution, time to goal.
7. **MoneyWise assistant** — a rule-based educational assistant that answers money
   questions using the user's own numbers.

## Target users

- Young South Africans (16–30) receiving a first salary or allowance
- Students on NSFAS or part-time income
- Unemployed people managing grants or household money
- First-time earners and gig/freelance workers
- Anyone who feels money "disappears" without understanding why

## Constraints and principles

- **Mobile-first**: the majority of the audience will use a phone.
- **Offline-capable**: core calculations run in the browser; the REST API adds
  persistence and shared logic when available.
- **South African context first**: taxi fares, data bundles, prepaid electricity,
  stokvels, funeral cover, family support, NSFAS, social grants.
- **Education, not advice**: MoneyWise SA explains and equips. It never gives
  regulated personalised financial advice.
- **No paywall on the basics**: budgeting, calculators and education stay free.

## Success looks like

- A user can answer "can I afford this?" with numbers instead of a guess.
- A user knows their Money Confidence Score and their weakest pillar.
- A user has at least one funded savings goal and an emergency fund plan.
- A user can explain the difference between saving and investing in one sentence.
